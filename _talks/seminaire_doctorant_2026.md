We introduce the $\rho_w$-weak dependence coefficient, a covariance-based notion operating on finite blocks. In stationary regime, $\rho_w$ coincides with classical $\rho$-mixing, but since it operates on finite blocks rather than semi-infinite $\sigma$-algebras, it does not require stationarity and dominates dependence coefficients based on Lipschitz test functions $(\eta, \tau, \theta)$. This block structure makes it directly compatible with a Poincaré inequality in the spirit of a Efron-Stein decomposition, leading to a Herbst argument. We establish an explicit Poincaré inequality, with a close form factor $1 + m \rho_w(r)$ which tends to $1$ - the i.i.d       setting - under geometric decay. We then derive concentration bounds of type Rosenthal - Fuk-Nagaev (unbounded regime, by the moment method), then, based on the tilted measure and entropy method and a $\rho_w^Q$-stability assumption, McDiarmid and Bernstein bounds. The approach reaches functionals beyond the separately-Lipschitz scope — quadratic forms, delayed contrasts, and degenerate U-statistics.

This talk was given at the *Séminaire des doctorants*, a biannual event at the SAMM laboratory.
